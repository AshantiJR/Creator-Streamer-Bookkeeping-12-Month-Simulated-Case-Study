# Creator and Streamer Bookkeeping: 12-Month Simulated Case Study

## Project Overview

This portfolio project demonstrates a complete 12-month bookkeeping cycle for a fictional content creator and streamer. The business receives income from multiple platforms and has recurring software, equipment, contractor, advertising, and operating expenses.

The purpose of this project is to show how I organize financial records, categorize transactions, reconcile revenue and bank activity, identify discrepancies, and prepare financial reports that make the business’s activity easier to understand.

## Business Problem

Creators and streamers may receive income from subscriptions, advertisements, sponsorships, merchandise, affiliate commissions, memberships, and direct support. The amounts deposited into a bank account may not match the gross revenue shown on each platform because fees are often deducted before payouts are issued.

Without organized records and regular reconciliation, it can be difficult for a creator to understand their actual revenue, expenses, and overall financial position.

## Project Objectives

- Create and maintain a realistic Chart of Accounts
- Categorize 12 months of simulated transactions
- Reconcile platform revenue to bank deposits
- Complete a bank reconciliation
- Document month-end adjustments
- Prepare an Income Statement
- Prepare a Balance Sheet
- Identify missing, duplicated, or incorrectly categorized transactions
- Present the results through clear financial reports and visual summaries

## Project Results

The completed project organizes and analyzes 12 months of simulated financial activity for a fictional creator and streamer with multiple revenue sources and operating expenses.

The final workbook includes:

- A complete 12-month categorized transaction ledger
- Revenue tracking across multiple creator income streams
- Creator-platform payout reconciliation
- Bank reconciliation
- Month-end adjustment documentation
- Income Statement and Balance Sheet reporting
- Automated duplicate and missing-information checks
- Financial dashboard and visual reporting
- Formula-driven controls used to verify that financial reports reconcile to the underlying transaction data

The completed reporting system provides a structured view of the fictional business's revenue, expenses, profitability, account balances, and data-quality exceptions while maintaining traceability back to the underlying transactions.
